title: "Like an L2 Switch, I Felt the Absence of 'Customer History'"
date: 2026-07-22
tags: [Career, Engineering, Troubleshooting, MSP]
---

I provide technical support and troubleshooting services for multiple clients. The workflow is much like an `L2 switch`. When an issue, like a packet, comes in, I quickly process it by analyzing and forwarding it to its destination, then move on to the next ticket. Through this process, encountering various cases has certainly improved my agility in problem-solving.

However, this approach had a clear limitation: the **'absence of customer history'**.

While one-off issues can be resolved quickly, encountering complex, intertwined problems sometimes takes a long time to find a solution. This is because there's a lack of essential context to identify the root cause, such as the system's initial setup environment, past change history, or architectural specificities. This wasn't so much an issue of the engineer's capability but a structural inefficiency caused by information silos. Ultimately, I found myself pondering that I was merely performing a mechanical role, perceiving and reacting to the system's state.

These concerns led to a contemplation of two alternative career paths.

### Alternative 1: Dedicated System Engineer (On-premise Specialist)

This role involves exclusively managing a single client's system.

- **Pros:**
    - **Deep Understanding:** From the architecture of a specific system to all its change history, one can truly become a 'living history'. This is a key competency that can significantly reduce downtime during outages.
    - **Proactive Operations:** Beyond simple `troubleshooting`, it's possible to act as a proactive partner, identifying potential issues in advance and improving the system.
- **Cons:**
    - **Potential for Technical Stagnation:** Once a service stabilizes, there's a risk of getting bogged down in repetitive maintenance tasks, reducing opportunities to acquire new technologies or diverse problem-solving experiences.

### Alternative 2: Managed Service Provider (MSP Engineer)

This role involves taking responsibility for a client's cloud infrastructure across the entire lifecycle of `build-maintain-troubleshoot`.

- **Pros:**
    - **Full-Lifecycle Experience:** Based on client business requirements, one directly designs and builds the infrastructure, creating its 'history' oneself. This provides complete ownership of the system.
    - **Balance of Breadth and Depth:** By managing multiple clients, one experiences diverse tech stacks and business domains, while also maintaining deep partnerships with each client and taking responsibility for the overall system.
    - **Alignment with Market Trends:** Unlike high initial investment `On-premise` environments, as more companies consider cloud adoption, the role and opportunities for MSPs continue to expand.

### Conclusion: From Reactive Technician to Proactive Partner

Through this contemplation, I realized that what I truly desired was not merely to change my work environment, but to transform the very nature of my work. It's an aspiration to grow beyond being a technician who 'reacts' to sporadic issues, to become a partner who understands the entire context of a system and 'proactively' drives improvements.

From this perspective, an MSP engineer is an attractive path that addresses the core problem of 'lack of history' while also allowing for continuous growth through diverse experiences. `Troubleshooting` will no longer be about fragmented problem-solving, but rather part of a continuous process of diagnosing and improving the health of the systems I build and manage.
