### Problem Definition

The recent introduction of generative AI within the development team has necessitated a fundamental re-evaluation of existing development workflows and the definition of engineers' roles. In the past, coding ability and implementation speed were considered core competencies for engineers, but with AI automating a significant portion of code generation, this paradigm is no longer valid. This was analyzed not merely as the emergence of a new tool, but as a significant event signaling a shift in the very nature of development and the criteria for evaluating its value. Confusion regarding the redefinition of roles was particularly observed among specialized engineers who had built deep technical expertise.

### Root Cause Analysis

The root cause of this phenomenon is the drastic lowering of technical barriers in the 'Implementation' phase by AI. Previously, a high level of programming knowledge and experience was essential for transforming ideas into actual code. However, now, given clear requirements, AI can generate a significant portion of the code.

Consequently, the development bottleneck has shifted from 'How to build it' to 'What to build and Why'. As a result, merely being able to write code quickly and accurately is no longer sufficient for differentiation, and the core value contributing to project success has begun to emerge elsewhere.

### Troubleshooting & Resolution

To address this paradigm shift, internal discussions were held to redefine the engineer's role from a 'Coder' to a 'Creative/Technical Director' who leads projects with a technical vision. This signifies a shift in perspective, viewing AI not as an irreplaceable competitor, but as a capable team member that maximizes productivity.

The newly defined core competencies for engineers are as follows:

1.  **Clear Vision and Architectural Design Capability**: The ability to precisely instruct AI on 'what to build' and to envision the overall system's big picture. This is the most crucial step in determining the direction of the final product.
2.  **Original Ideas and Problem-Solving Skills**: In an environment where technology is standardized, gaining a competitive edge comes from novel ideas or creative approaches to complex problems that haven't existed before.
3.  **High-Level Technical Acumen and Quality Assurance**: The critical thinking ability to select the most technically superior and efficient code from numerous AI-generated outputs, identify potential bugs or structural issues, and propose directions for improvement. Existing deep specialized knowledge becomes a powerful weapon at this stage.
4.  **Persistence through Continuous Questioning and Improvement**: The ability to guide AI by constantly seeking better outcomes, never settling for the status quo, asking questions like 'Is this the best it can be?' or 'Is there a better structure?'

In conclusion, the engineer's role must evolve to be liberated from low-level coding tasks, focusing instead on high-value activities such as setting project direction, validating AI-generated outputs, and taking responsibility for the final quality. This is interpreted not as technology replacing humans, but as a process of providing more powerful tools to skilled engineers, thereby amplifying their capabilities.
